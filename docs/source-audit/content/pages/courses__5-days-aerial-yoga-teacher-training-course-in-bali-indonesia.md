5 Days Aerial Yoga Teacher Training Course in Bali, Indonesia

Aerial yoga, sometimes called anti-gravity or flying yoga, is among the most inventive and exciting expressions of yoga practiced today. It is a blend of classical asana and some elements of acrobatics and suspended movement with a silk hammock that is tightly secured at a high level. This practice is a reconstruction of your relationship in a balanced, patient and conscious way, and creates a strong communication between the body and the mind that most traditional practices fail to establish.

Aerial yoga is not done on a mat, unlike traditional yoga. This is done above the ground level and hangs from the ceiling in fabric secured tightly to it. This one-rotation makes the whole experience really therapeutic and beneficial to the body, mind and overall sense of well-being.

What Is Aerial Yoga and the Aerial Yoga Hammock?

The hammock is considered the foundation of aerial yoga, so if you're looking to learn more, you need to begin with the hammock.

The fabric is surprisingly simple, soft, smooth and feels great against the skin, but designed to support a person's entire body weight without stress or harm. Instructors alter the hammock before every class, this isn't a standard frame it's set up to fit each student so it's not the same one for everyone but rather it's for you.

In fact, the hammock does more than just provide support. It provides room for inversion which the floor just can't beat. It enables the body to go deeper than it would go on its own to stretch and rotate.

What Typically Happens During an Aerial Yoga Class

The teacher encourages students to align, adjust and find the breath in each aerial yoga session. Safety and confidence are of paramount importance throughout, and every class is made to feel good, positive and progressive.

01
Warm-Up

The class starts with simple stretches, breathing and movement to prepare the body to move in the air. Light spinal movement and shoulder openers help the body feel awake before the fabric is added.

02
Hammock Set-Up

Each student sets up their hammock at the right height for their body. Instructors stay close by to ensure everything is correctly and safely adjusted before practice begins.

03
Guided Postures and Flows

The teacher leads the group through a well-organised sequence of poses and flows, with clear transitions and variations for different levels.

04
Aerial Inversions

Students practice partial or full inversions while the spine decompresses and the body experiences a deep sense of release with full hammock support.

05
Deep Stretching

Students use the hammock for positions that may feel more difficult on the floor, especially stretches for the hips, chest and spine.

06
Savasana and Relaxation

Savasana is practiced in the hammock at the end of each session. Students leave feeling peaceful, refreshed and deeply relaxed.
