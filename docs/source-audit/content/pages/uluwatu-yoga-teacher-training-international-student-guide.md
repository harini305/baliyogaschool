Uluwatu Yoga Teacher Training in Bali: International Student Guide
YOGA OCTOBER 2, 2026 BALI YOGA SCHOOL  SHARE

Uluwatu sits on the southern tip of Bali, and it has quickly become one of the most loved spots for yoga teacher training. With its dramatic cliffs, world-class surf breaks, and laid-back atmosphere, the area offers something different from the usual yoga destinations. For international students planning their first trip here, though, a lot of questions come up before booking a course.

Where will you stay? What will you eat? Is the area safe and easy to get around? This guide answers all of that. We have put together everything international students need to know before starting their yoga teacher training journey in Uluwatu.

Why Uluwatu Stands Out Among Bali’s Yoga Destinations

Bali has several popular areas for yoga, including Ubud and Canggu. So, why do many students choose Uluwatu instead?

The answer lies in its unique balance. Uluwatu offers a quieter, more grounded feel compared to the busier tourist areas. At the same time, it has enough cafes, beaches, and activities to keep things interesting outside of class hours.

The cliffside views alone make a strong case for choosing this location. Many BYS students say that practicing yoga with ocean views completely changes how they feel during training. The natural beauty here does a lot of the calming work that yoga aims to achieve.

Uluwatu is also a popular destination for digital nomads, surfers, and wellness tourists. This makes it easier for international students to make friends and feel comfortable, even when traveling alone, in a diversified and welcoming community.

One more element to note is the space and atmosphere. Ubud and the surrounding area of Canggu have developed quite rapidly in recent years, and during the high tourist season it is possible to experience traffic congestion here. Uluwatu, however, retains more of the laid-back vibe with its spacious streets, clear blue skies, and less of the crowd pushing and shoving through the narrow lanes.

This is significant for students who would like their training to be more of a retreat than a stay in a busy city. The mornings often here begin with the birds singing and the ocean breeze, instead of the noise of traffic, which sets a more relaxed tone for the rest of the day of practice.

Accommodation Options for International Students

Accommodation is one of the major issues when students are traveling from abroad. Fortunately, there are a number of great options available at Uluwatu depending on budget and comfort level.

1. Residential Training Accommodation

Students may attend Residential Yoga Teacher Training at BYS and stay at the school for the entire training. This reduces the stress as students do not have to look for separate housing or be concerned of commuting daily to school.

Rooms are basic, clean and comfortable. The majority of students live in two or three bedrooms with other students and that really helps to make friends easily. The residential experience includes shared space, shared areas, and quiet study areas.

This is a great solution for international students. You don’t have to wander around at night, or find out how to get around in unfamiliar locations just to make it to class. It’s all within walking distance.

 

2. Independent Accommodation Nearby

Some students would like to have more independence while at school. There are a number of uluwatu guesthouses, homestays and small villas within the vicinity of most of the yoga schools at Uluwatu.

The prices can range, from basic rooms to fees for more expensive private villas with pools. If students select this option, they should reserve several weeks ahead of time, particularly the summer months (May through September).

In either case, comfort needs to be a factor in the decision. Teacher training days begin early in the day and involve physical practice, a good night’s sleep and a tranquil environment will make a difference to their energy levels throughout the course.

 

3. Things to Check Before Booking

No matter which one you select, there are some minor details that could save you a lot of trouble later. Make sure that air conditioning, hot water and a reliable WiFi connection are provided, as they are not always available in Uluwatu properties.

It also helps to read reviews by previous customers, especially by other yoga students – if you can. They frequently talk about the noise levels and the distance from the training location, and how helpful the staff are with the questions they have on an everyday basis. Some initial investigation, and a much more pleasant stay.

Food and Dining During Your Stay

Most people don’t associate food with yoga training, but it does. Food directly influences energy levels, digestion and concentration for long periods of practice.

Meals During Residential Training

Students enrolled in the Residential Yoga Teacher Training program get a fresh, healthy meal, as part of the course. Foods are planned to be light, nutritious and non-fatigue inducing for meals to focus on physical practice.

There will be a ton of fruits and veggies, rice dishes, and plant protein. Training hours food is based on the yogic principles, which means that heavy, processed or too spicy foods are typically not encouraged for meals.

This building is especially suitable for students from overseas. When you move to another country you already have a lot of changes to deal with. With reliable, healthy meals being removed, there’s one less planning obstacle.

Exploring Local Food Options

There is a wonderful selection of food outside of the scheduled training meals at Uluwatu. The beachfront cafes offer fresh seafood, smoothie bowls and international comfort foods.

Students like to visit local warungs – small family-run restaurants that offer authentic Indonesian food at low prices. Eating nasi goreng, gado-gado or fresh coconut water is an integral part of the Bali experience.

It’s also great for those who have dietary restrictions. There are plenty of vegan, vegetarian and gluten-free choices, as many people who prefer a vegan, a vegetarian or gluten-free diet are already residents of the region.

There’s a lot to enjoy for coffee lovers too. Located Uluwatu is experiencing a cafe culture, where numerous places offer Balinese coffee from local plantations, fresh pastries, and nutritious breakfast bowls. By the cliffs many students gather before braving the early morning hours of the weekend, with a hot beverage in hand, to head off for an afternoon of practice or sightseeing.

Water for consumption should be mentioned as well. Students should only drink bottled or filtered water while in Bali as the water is not potable directly. There are filtered water stations in most of the accommodations and training centres, which helps to reduce the single use plastic waste during the trip.

Getting Around Uluwatu: Travel Tips for International Students

The first-time visitor is usually concerned about transportation as much as anything else, but once you know the details about getting around Uluwatu, it should be relatively easy.

Airport to Uluwatu: The most common airport for international students is Ngurah Rai International Airport in Denpasar, which is approximately a 30 to 45-minute drive from Uluwatu, depending on traffic. Signing up for an airport transfer in advance is ideal for a stress-free arrival, and many students book through BYS in advance. This will eliminate taxi fare negotiations after a long flight and make travel hassle-free and seamless from the gate up.

 

Getting Around on a Scooter: Once settled in, scooters remain the most popular way to get around Uluwatu. The renting of one is relatively inexpensive and provides full freedom for students to explore beaches, cafes and viewpoints at their own time. But the roads are narrower, and the traffic is sometimes unpredictable, as opposed to what students are accustomed to when they are back home, so pour yourselves a cup of coffee, wear a helmet, and ride slowly and easily on new roads at night can save lives.

 

Alternatives to Scooters: If students are not interested in scooting, ride-hailing apps and local drivers will provide a convenient and hassle-free way to navigate. For those who have not yet fully mastered the local roads or prefer a different type of transportation between destinations while on vacation, many guesthouses and training centres will be able to book trusted drivers for day trips or regular errands.

 

Currency and Everyday Costs: Indonesia’s currency is the Indonesian Rupiah, and although the use of cards is gaining ground, cash is still more convenient for the smaller warungs, the local markets and for hiring scooters. Uluwatu ATM cash withdrawals are easy to do, but it’s wise to check with your home bank before you get started to prevent any unpleasant surprises. These are also fairly inexpensive on a daily basis when compared to a lot of western countries; students can enjoy their time here without having to constantly be conscious of their finances.
Preparing for Your Yoga Teacher Training Course

When travel and living arrangements are taken care of, the next important step is course preparation. Slight preparation prevents a lot of problems!

1. Physical Preparation

Experience and advanced flexibility are not necessary to join students. But, with a bit of strength and stamina built up before making the transition to daily practice is more comfortable.

Twenty minutes a day at home, even over the course of a few weeks prior to the course, will make a difference. Practice core poses, basic breath work and gentle stretching to ease the body slowly.

 

2. Mental and Emotional Readiness

Yoga teacher training is not just physical exercise. After extended practice time, philosophy lessons, and introspection exercises can arise emotions that were not anticipated.

It does help to have an open mind when approaching the course. Students who come with a focus on personal development, in addition to physical fitness, often share more substantial and fulfilling experiences at the end.

 

3. Practical Packing Tips

International students are advised to bring light, airy, and appropriately warm and humid clothes that will be acceptable in Bali. Yoga clothes, a yoga mat (if you have a favourite) and simple clothes for cultural outings are all on the packing list.

Sunscreen and insect repellents as well as a reusable water bottle are other items that make life a little easier on a daily basis. Because Uluwatu is near the coast, humidity levels remain fairly high all year long, so quick dry fabrics are better than heavier fabrics.

 

4. Documents and Logistics

Students should check visa requirements prior to travelling according to their nationalities and the duration of their stay. There are plenty of countries that can obtain a visa through the Indonesian Embassy upon arrival, although the list is subject to changes from time to time, it is always better to get to know the current country requirements in advance to avoid potential last-minute hassles.

It’s also a good idea to get travel insurance, particularly because there’s a lot of physical activity involved in yoga training and outdoor pursuits such as surfing or hiking that many students also enjoy during their trips.

What Makes BYS Training in Uluwatu Different

It can be difficult for students to determine what makes one school better than another, given the amount of information available to them. At Bali Yoga School, the focus stays on creating a complete experience, not just a certification.

Training programmes are a mix of yogic teachings and modern, practical teaching. During the course students will obtain Yoga Certification Bali accreditation, recognised globally and gain hands-on experience teaching.

Personal attention to all students by experienced teachers is provided due to small class sizes. The environment of the school with the combined impact of the residential setting, fresh food and nature of Uluwatu contribute to good concentrated and meaningful learning.

This is especially advantageous for international students. All rooms, food and training combined under one roof, which eliminates unnecessary stress, and focus can be placed on the training itself.

Final Thoughts

Taking the decision to study yoga teacher training abroad is a significant one, particularly for first time international travellers. But Uluwatu offers a smoother roadmap to this change than others, with a friendly community, beautiful natural scenery, and convenient facilities geared toward wellness tourism.

Bali Yoga School still attracts students from all over the world to its Uluwatu courses annually. If you’re thinking about taking this route, Uluwatu isn’t just a place to train, it’s a place to grow as a person, on and off the mat.

So if you’ve decided you’re ready to start your yoga teacher training course in one of the most beautiful places on earth, BYS is here to help you start planning today.
